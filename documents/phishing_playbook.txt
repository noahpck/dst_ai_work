# PLAYBOOK 4: Phishing / Business Email Compromise (BEC)
Framework: NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 2

## DETECTION SIGNALS:
- User reports a suspicious email requesting credentials, payment, or sensitive information
- Multiple users receive similar phishing emails within a short period
- Email contains malicious attachments or links to unfamiliar domains
- Unusual login activity following email interaction (e.g., impossible travel, unfamiliar IP addresses)
- Email authentication failures (SPF, DKIM, or DMARC)
- Security tools detect known phishing indicators or malicious URLs

## ANALYSIS:
- Identify the sender, recipient(s), subject, and timestamp of the email
- Examine email headers to verify the true source and identify spoofing
- Analyze attachments and embedded URLs in a secure sandbox environment
- Determine whether any users clicked links, opened attachments, or submitted credentials
- Identify compromised accounts and assess whether lateral phishing or unauthorized mailbox activity occurred

## CONTAINMENT:
- Quarantine or remove the phishing email from all affected mailboxes
- Disable or lock compromised user accounts
- Reset passwords and revoke active authentication sessions
- Block malicious domains, URLs, sender addresses, and IP addresses at the email gateway and firewall
- Isolate any endpoint that executed malicious attachments or payloads

## ERADICATION:
- Remove malware or unauthorized software installed as a result of the phishing attack
- Delete malicious email rules, forwarding rules, or inbox rules created by the attacker
- Scan affected systems for persistence mechanisms and additional compromise
- Patch exploited software and update email security signatures
- Verify that no additional accounts or systems have been compromised

## POST-INCIDENT:
- Notify affected users and provide guidance on recognizing future phishing attempts
- Conduct a review of the attack timeline and response effectiveness
- Update email filtering policies and phishing detection rules based on observed indicators
- Perform organization-wide phishing awareness training if necessary
- Document indicators of compromise (IOCs), lessons learned, and recommendations for future prevention
